1 · RCRSP, Rotator Cuff Tear, Frozen Shoulder & GH OAMATRIX A
Subacromial pain — definition Kulkarni 2015 (Guideline): “commonly located to the top and lateral side of the shoulder … exaggerated by overhead activity … can be associated with night pain … usually associated with full passive range of movement”
RCRSP terminology & burden: umbrella term for subacromial pain, RC tendinopathy, partial and full-thickness tears; exercise ≈ surgery (Lewis 2016 Man Ther) E • 2.4% of UK primary care consultations; subacromial pain up to 70% of shoulder-pain problems (Beard 2018 Lancet CSAW) M
Full-thickness rotator cuff tear: “overall prevalence of rotator cuff tears is 34% and … risk increases significantly with age” (Kulkarni 2015, Guideline); US cluster Sp 98% (94–100), LR+ 6.2 (1.5–25.7) (Cadogan 2016) M
Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis): global active & passive restriction; ROM loss > 25% in at least 2 planes AND passive ER loss > 50% of the other side or < 30° ER (Kelley 2013 JOSPT CPG, Guideline)
Glenohumeral osteoarthritis (GH OA): those with ER loss had more GH arthropathy on X-ray (17% vs 3%) (Cadogan 2016 PLoS One) M
2 · Instability, AC Joint, SLAP/Biceps, Calcific & Cervical/TOSMATRIX B
Traumatic instability — recurrence Brownson 2015 (Guideline): almost 90% of recurrences within 2 years; males under 20 ≈ 72% chance of recurrent instability; immobilisation > 1 week does not reduce recurrence
Atraumatic instability (Stanmore II/III) Noorani 2019 (Guideline): Stanmore polar types II (hyperlaxity) and III (muscle patterning, structurally normal)
AC joint pathology: superior AC pain & high-arc provocation; no impingement or AC-joint test showed significant diagnostic accuracy (2008 meta-analysis) (Hegedus 2008 BJSM) M
SLAP / long head of biceps: deep mechanical clicking/pain; pooled DOR of physical tests 1.38 (1.13–1.69) — poor (Gismervik 2017 BMC Musculoskelet Disord) M
Calcific tendinopathy: acute or chronic calcific tendinitis of the rotator cuff with severe flare pain (see ESWT & barbotage on p6) E
Cervical radiculopathy / Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (TOS): cervical screen (Wainner cluster, p1) M and TOS neurovascular upper-limb symptoms E
3 · Frozen Shoulder Box (Kelley 2013 JOSPT CPG) Kelley 2013GUIDELINE
Definition used (Guideline): ROM loss > 25% in at least 2 planes AND passive ER loss > 50% of the other side or < 30° ER
Prevalence & risk factors (Guideline): shoulder pain 2.4–26%; primary adhesive capsulitis 2% to 5.3%; secondary (diabetes/thyroid) 4.3%–38%; diabetes in women 23.7% vs 4.7% and men 38.0% vs 6.5%; hypothyroidism (women) 21.1% vs 7.9%; age 40–65, peak 51–55; contralateral risk 5%–34%, bilateral simultaneous up to 14%
4 clinical stages (Guideline): stage 1 up to 3 months; stage 2 “freezing” 3–9 months; stage 3 “frozen” 9–15 months; stage 4 “thawing” 15–24 months; mild symptoms may persist for years

4 · Cadogan Passive ER Rule & Cluster · Hanchard CochranePRIMARY CARE
Subacromial pain vs stiff shoulder (Cadogan 2016 PLoS One) M: full passive ER: Sn 99%, LR− 0.14 → loss of passive ER argues against subacromial pain; those with ER loss had more GH arthropathy on X-ray (17% vs 3%)
Cadogan subacromial pain cluster (Cadogan 2016) M: anterior shoulder pain + strain injury + no symptoms at end-range ER in abduction: Sp 100% (LR+ ∞, 2.9–∞); full-thickness supraspinatus tear on US: Sp 98% (94–100), LR+ 6.2 (1.5–25.7); 34% had a positive subacromial anaesthetic response
