1 · ESWT & Barbotage (Calcific vs Non-Calcific) BANNURU · SURACE · MOOSMAYER
BESS on ESWT Kulkarni 2015 (Guideline): “Extracorporeal shock wave therapy may improve pain in calcific tendonitis.”
ESWT, calcific tendinitis (MA, 28 RCTs) (Bannuru 2014 Ann Intern Med) M: high-energy ESWT better than placebo for pain, function and calcium resorption in calcific tendinitis; no difference vs placebo in non-calcific tendinitis
ESWT (Cochrane, 32 trials) (Surace 2020 Cochrane) M: vs placebo at 3 months: pain 0.78 better (0.17–1.4) on 0–10, below the 1.5 clinically important change; function 7.9 better (1.6–14), below 10 → benefit may not be clinically important
Barbotage (lavage) for calcific tendinopathy (RCT, n = 218) (Moosmayer 2023 BMJ) H: OSS at 4 months: lavage + steroid vs sham 0.2 (−2.3 to 2.8); none superior to sham at 24 months
2 · Electrotherapy (Cochrane) & Lafrance 2022 CPG PAGE 2016 · LAFRANCE 2022
Electrotherapy (Cochrane, 47 trials) (Page 2016 Cochrane) L: no meta-analysis possible; pulsed ultrasound for calcific tendinitis (1 trial, 61 participants, low quality): pain MD −8.60 (−13.48 to −3.72) on a 52-point scale
JOSPT/Quebec CPG 2022 (cuff disorders) Lafrance 2022 (Guideline): “Clinicians should prescribe an active and task-oriented rehabilitation program (exercises and education)”; “Subacromial decompression is not recommended to treat rotator cuff tendinopathy”; injections “may be useful to reduce pain in the short term”
3 · Corticosteroid Injections & Hydrodilatation (Cuff Tendinosis vs Frozen Shoulder) MOHAMADI · CHALLOUMAS · CARETTE · KELLEY · BUCHBINDER
Corticosteroid injection, cuff tendinosis (MA) (Mohamadi 2017 Clin Orthop Relat Res) M: no better than placebo at 3 months; small transient relief at 4–8 weeks (SMD 0.52); at least 5 treated for 1 to benefit (see p5 Forsythe 2019 if repair planned within 1 month: infection 1.3% vs 0.8%, OR 1.7, 1.0–2.9)
Frozen shoulder treatments (NMA, 65 studies) (Challoumas 2020 JAMA Netw Open) M: only intra-articular corticosteroid clinically superior short term: pain vs placebo MD −1.0 (−1.5 to −0.5), vs physio −1.1 (−1.7 to −0.5) VAS
Frozen shoulder: injection ± physio (RCT) (Carette 2003 Arthritis Rheum) M: injection + home exercise effective; adding supervised physio gives faster ROM gain; physio alone of limited efficacy; all groups similar at 12 months
JOSPT CPG — injection Kelley 2013 (Guideline): “Intra-articular corticosteroid injections combined with shoulder mobility and stretching exercises are more effective in providing short-term (4-6 weeks) pain relief and improved function compared to shoulder mobility and stretching exercises alone.” (strong evidence)
Hydrodilatation / arthrographic distension (Cochrane) (Buchbinder 2008 Cochrane) L: 1 low-risk-of-bias trial: distension with saline + steroid better than placebo at 3 weeks (NNTB 2 pain, 3 function)
