How to perform

  1. Perform Upper Limb Tension Test A (ULTT A — median nerve bias) as the primary high-sensitivity screening test to rule out cervical radiculopathy.
  2. Measure active ipsilateral cervical rotation range of motion (< 60° threshold).
  3. Perform the seated cervical distraction test (symptom reduction) and Spurling test (radicular symptom reproduction).
  4. Count the number of positive tests across the 4-item cluster.

Interpretation

When all 4 items are positive, LR+ rises to 30.3 (wide CI); a negative ULTT A is the most useful single test to rule out cervical radiculopathy.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: High

Hopewell 2021 Lancet (GRASP, n = 708, Level H): progressive exercise vs one best-practice advice session: SPADI over 12 months −0.66 (99% CI −4.52 to 3.20) — no difference; injection vs no injection −1.11 (−4.47 to 2.26). Holmgren 2012 BMJ (Level H): Constant-Murley +24 vs +9 points (MD 15, 8.5–20.6); surgery chosen by 20% vs 63% (OR 7.7, 3.1–19.4). Littlewood 2016 Clin Rehabil (SELF, Level M): SPADI at 3 months 32.4 vs 30.7 usual physio; adjusted MD 3.2 (−6.0 to 12.4) — comparable. Smith 2017 BJSM (MA, Level M): short-term pain SMD −0.27 (−0.54 to −0.05) in favour of painful exercise; no difference later. Saito 2018 Physiother Theory Pract (MA, 6 RCTs, Level M): short-term pain MD −0.88 (−1.19 to −0.58), function −11.31 (−17.20 to −5.41); no difference at follow-up.

Return-to-sport testing battery for upper-limb athletes including closed kinetic chain, reach symmetry, power, isometric cuff capacity, and psychological readiness.

Key points

  1. Administer Closed Kinetic Chain Upper Extremity Stability Test (CKCUEST): Measure touches in 15 seconds across 3 trials; SEM 1.45–2.76 touches, MDC 2.05–3.91, at least 3 touches = real improvement (Tucci 2014); 21 touches: Sn 0.83, Sp 0.79, OR 18.75 (Pontillo 2014).
  2. Administer Upper Quarter Y-Balance Test (YBT-UQ): Medial, inferolateral, and superolateral reach distances; dominant and non-dominant limbs similar — uninjured limb serves as normal (Westrick 2012).
  3. Administer Seated Single-Arm Shot Put: Measure throw distance; limb symmetry index exceeded 100 % in healthy athletes (males 108.7 %, females 104.4 %) (Chmielewski 2014).
  4. Administer Athletic Shoulder (ASH) test: Prone I, Y, and T positions; interday ICC 0.94–0.98, MDC 13.2 and 25.9 N (Ashworth 2018); ICC 0.77–0.99 (Królikowska 2022); intra-day ICC 0.908–0.969 (Çiftçi 2025); use one rater as only non-dominant T has excellent inter-rater reliability (Schellekens 2025); dominant stronger in T and Y (Trunt 2022).
  5. Administer Shoulder Instability-Return to Sport after Injury (SIRSI) scale: Psychological readiness; internal consistency α = 0.96, test–retest ρ = 0.93; RTS rugby score 60.9 ± 26.6 % vs 38.1 ± 25.6 % (Gerometta 2018).

Positive / result

Objective attainment of symmetry and normative thresholds before discharge or athletic clearance.

Interpretation

Comprehensive upper-limb RTS assessment combines functional stability (CKCUEST: Tucci 2014, Pontillo 2014), dynamic balance (YBT-UQ: Westrick 2012), explosive power (Shot Put: Chmielewski 2014), isometric cuff force (ASH test: Ashworth 2018, Królikowska 2022, Çiftçi 2025, Schellekens 2025, Trunt 2022), and psychological readiness (SIRSI: Gerometta 2018).

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Moderate

CKCUEST MDC 2.05–3.91, ≥ 3 touches real change (Tucci 2014); YBT-UQ symmetry (Westrick 2012); Shot put LSI > 100 % (Chmielewski 2014); ASH test ICC 0.94–0.98, MDC 13.2–25.9 N (Ashworth 2018); SIRSI α = 0.96, ρ = 0.93 (Gerometta 2018)
