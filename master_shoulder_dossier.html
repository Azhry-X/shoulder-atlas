Differential Diagnosis, Frozen Shoulder & Clinical Clusters

Differential & stiff-vs-mobile stratification: first compare passive external rotation (Cadogan 2016 / Kelley 2013), classify instability (Brownson 2015 / Noorani 2019), and clear the cervical spine and thoracic outlet.
1 · RCRSP, Rotator Cuff Tear, Frozen Shoulder & GH OAMATRIX A
Subacromial pain — definition Kulkarni 2015 (Guideline): “commonly located to the top and lateral side of the shoulder … exaggerated by overhead activity … can be associated with night pain … usually associated with full passive range of movement”
RCRSP terminology & burden: umbrella term for subacromial pain, RC tendinopathy, partial and full-thickness tears; exercise ≈ surgery (Lewis 2016 Man Ther) E • 2.4% of UK primary care consultations; subacromial pain up to 70% of shoulder-pain problems (Beard 2018 Lancet CSAW) M
Full-thickness rotator cuff tear: “overall prevalence of rotator cuff tears is 34% and … risk increases significantly with age” (Kulkarni 2015, Guideline); US cluster Sp 98% (94–100), LR+ 6.2 (1.5–25.7) (Cadogan 2016) M
Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis): global active & passive restriction; ROM loss > 25% in at least 2 planes AND passive ER loss > 50% of the other side or < 30° ER (Kelley 2013 JOSPT CPG, Guideline)
Glenohumeral osteoarthritis (GH OA): those with ER loss had more GH arthropathy on X-ray (17% vs 3%) (Cadogan 2016 PLoS One) M
2 · Instability, AC Joint, SLAP/Biceps, Calcific & Cervical/TOSMATRIX B
Traumatic instability — recurrence Brownson 2015 (Guideline): almost 90% of recurrences within 2 years; males under 20 ≈ 72% chance of recurrent instability; immobilisation > 1 week does not reduce recurrence
Atraumatic instability (Stanmore II/III) Noorani 2019 (Guideline): Stanmore polar types II (hyperlaxity) and III (muscle patterning, structurally normal)
AC joint pathology: superior AC pain & high-arc provocation; no impingement or AC-joint test showed significant diagnostic accuracy (2008 meta-analysis) (Hegedus 2008 BJSM) M
SLAP / long head of biceps: deep mechanical clicking/pain; pooled DOR of physical tests 1.38 (1.13–1.69) — poor (Gismervik 2017 BMC Musculoskelet Disord) M
Calcific tendinopathy: acute or chronic calcific tendinitis of the rotator cuff with severe flare pain (see ESWT & barbotage on p6) E
Cervical radiculopathy / Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (TOS): cervical screen (Wainner cluster, p1) M and TOS neurovascular upper-limb symptoms E
3 · Frozen Shoulder Box (Kelley 2013 JOSPT CPG) Kelley 2013GUIDELINE
Definition used (Guideline): ROM loss > 25% in at least 2 planes AND passive ER loss > 50% of the other side or < 30° ER
Prevalence & risk factors (Guideline): shoulder pain 2.4–26%; primary adhesive capsulitis 2% to 5.3%; secondary (diabetes/thyroid) 4.3%–38%; diabetes in women 23.7% vs 4.7% and men 38.0% vs 6.5%; hypothyroidism (women) 21.1% vs 7.9%; age 40–65, peak 51–55; contralateral risk 5%–34%, bilateral simultaneous up to 14%
4 clinical stages (Guideline): stage 1 up to 3 months; stage 2 “freezing” 3–9 months; stage 3 “frozen” 9–15 months; stage 4 “thawing” 15–24 months; mild symptoms may persist for years

Injection before cuff repair (Forsythe 2019 JBJS Am) M: injection within 1 month before arthroscopic cuff repair: infection 1.3% vs 0.8% (OR 1.7, 1.0–2.9)
Suspected post-op joint infection: “A suspected infected joint needs same day emergency referral.” (Kulkarni 2015 BESS/BOA, Guideline) • screen for erythema, purulent drainage, fever, or escalating rest pain E
VISIT RE-CHECK: Portals / wound clean & dry (no infection) Axillary & distal neurovascular status intact No DVT / systemic fever signs Surgeon restrictions verified
2 · Rotator Cuff Repair PROM Timing KLUCZYNSKI 2015
Cuff repair: early vs delayed PROM (MA, 28 studies, 1,729 repairs) (Kluczynski 2015 Am J Sports Med) M: level 1 studies: retear 13.7% early vs 10.5% delayed, RR 1.30 (0.74–2.30), P = .36; tears ≤ 3 cm (TO + single-row): 18.7% vs 28.2%, RR 0.66 (0.47–0.95); tears > 5 cm (double-row): 56.4% vs 20%, RR 2.82 (1.31–6.07) → tear size matters
Restrictions: POST-OP RESTRICTIONS ARE SURGEON-DIRECTED E
3 · Bankart vs Latarjet & Return to Sport HOSSEIN ZADEH · MEMON
Bankart vs Latarjet (MA, 21 studies, 13,176 shoulders) (Hossein Zadeh 2024 J Shoulder Elbow Surg) M: arthroscopic Bankart RR 3.08 (2.03–4.68) for recurrence/revision vs Latarjet; Rowe +4.55 for Latarjet; total complications ≈ 47% lower with Bankart (RR 0.53, 0.31–0.90)
Return to sport after arthroscopic Bankart (SR, 34 studies) (Memon 2018 J Shoulder Elbow Surg) M: any sport 81% (74–87%); pre-injury level 66% (57–74%); ≈ one-third do not return to pre-injury level
Restrictions: POST-OP RESTRICTIONS ARE SURGEON-DIRECTED E
4 · Subacromial Decompression & MID / PASS CSAW · FIMPACT · KANTO
CSAW (n = 313) (Beard 2018 Lancet) H: OSS at 6 months: decompression 32.7 vs arthroscopy only 34.2, MD −1.3 (−3.9 to 1.3); both surgical groups only a small benefit over no treatment (2.8 points vs decompression)
FIMPACT (24 months) (Paavola 2018 BMJ) H: ASD vs diagnostic arthroscopy: pain VAS −4.6 (−11.3 to 2.1) at rest, −9.0 (−18.1 to 0.2) on activity — no clinically relevant difference

MID / PASS after subacromial surgery (Kanto 2021 BMC Med Res Methodol) M: MID: pain on activity 20 mm, Constant 10 points, SST 1.5; PASS: Constant 80, SST 11

1 · ESWT & Barbotage (Calcific vs Non-Calcific) BANNURU · SURACE · MOOSMAYER
BESS on ESWT Kulkarni 2015 (Guideline): “Extracorporeal shock wave therapy may improve pain in calcific tendonitis.”
ESWT, calcific tendinitis (MA, 28 RCTs) (Bannuru 2014 Ann Intern Med) M: high-energy ESWT better than placebo for pain, function and calcium resorption in calcific tendinitis; no difference vs placebo in non-calcific tendinitis
ESWT (Cochrane, 32 trials) (Surace 2020 Cochrane) M: vs placebo at 3 months: pain 0.78 better (0.17–1.4) on 0–10, below the 1.5 clinically important change; function 7.9 better (1.6–14), below 10 → benefit may not be clinically important
Barbotage (lavage) for calcific tendinopathy (RCT, n = 218) (Moosmayer 2023 BMJ) H: OSS at 4 months: lavage + steroid vs sham 0.2 (−2.3 to 2.8); none superior to sham at 24 months
2 · Electrotherapy (Cochrane) & Lafrance 2022 CPG PAGE 2016 · LAFRANCE 2022
Electrotherapy (Cochrane, 47 trials) (Page 2016 Cochrane) L: no meta-analysis possible; pulsed ultrasound for calcific tendinitis (1 trial, 61 participants, low quality): pain MD −8.60 (−13.48 to −3.72) on a 52-point scale
JOSPT/Quebec CPG 2022 (cuff disorders) Lafrance 2022 (Guideline): “Clinicians should prescribe an active and task-oriented rehabilitation program (exercises and education)”; “Subacromial decompression is not recommended to treat rotator cuff tendinopathy”; injections “may be useful to reduce pain in the short term”
3 · Corticosteroid Injections & Hydrodilatation (Cuff Tendinosis vs Frozen Shoulder) MOHAMADI · CHALLOUMAS · CARETTE · KELLEY · BUCHBINDER
Corticosteroid injection, cuff tendinosis (MA) (Mohamadi 2017 Clin Orthop Relat Res) M: no better than placebo at 3 months; small transient relief at 4–8 weeks (SMD 0.52); at least 5 treated for 1 to benefit (see p5 Forsythe 2019 if repair planned within 1 month: infection 1.3% vs 0.8%, OR 1.7, 1.0–2.9)
Frozen shoulder treatments (NMA, 65 studies) (Challoumas 2020 JAMA Netw Open) M: only intra-articular corticosteroid clinically superior short term: pain vs placebo MD −1.0 (−1.5 to −0.5), vs physio −1.1 (−1.7 to −0.5) VAS
Frozen shoulder: injection ± physio (RCT) (Carette 2003 Arthritis Rheum) M: injection + home exercise effective; adding supervised physio gives faster ROM gain; physio alone of limited efficacy; all groups similar at 12 months
JOSPT CPG — injection Kelley 2013 (Guideline): “Intra-articular corticosteroid injections combined with shoulder mobility and stretching exercises are more effective in providing short-term (4-6 weeks) pain relief and improved function compared to shoulder mobility and stretching exercises alone.” (strong evidence)
Hydrodilatation / arthrographic distension (Cochrane) (Buchbinder 2008 Cochrane) L: 1 low-risk-of-bias trial: distension with saline + steroid better than placebo at 3 weeks (NNTB 2 pain, 3 function)
